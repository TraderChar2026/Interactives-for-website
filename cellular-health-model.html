The Cellular Health Model

How modifiable inputs influence cellular stress, function, and resilience.

Four Areas We Can Influence

Nutrition, the microbiome, cellular pathway activation, and lifestyle and environmental exposures all influence cellular function. Genetics and age help determine the starting point, but these four areas provide opportunities for intervention.

Oxidative Stress Is a Balance

All four areas influence the balance between reactive oxygen species and the cell’s antioxidant defenses. The goal is not to eliminate reactive oxygen species—they are also important signaling molecules—but to maintain the cell’s ability to keep oxidative stress under control.

Oxidative Stress and Inflammation Reinforce Each Other

Excess oxidative stress can activate inflammatory pathways such as NF-κB and increase cytokine signaling. Inflammation, in turn, can generate additional reactive oxygen species, creating a cycle that increases cellular stress.

Cells Need Defense, Energy and Repair

These are different but interconnected functions. Nrf2 supports cellular defense. Nrf1 supports mitochondrial renewal. NAD+ supports energy metabolism and repair.

Together with cellular cleanup systems such as autophagy and mitophagy, these pathways help cells defend themselves, produce energy, remove damage, and recover.

How to Use This Model

Read downward to understand what influences cellular health. Nutrition, the microbiome, pathway activation, and lifestyle and environmental exposures influence oxidative stress and inflammation. These, in turn, affect the cell’s ability to defend itself, produce energy, and repair damage.

Read upward to identify opportunities for intervention. When cellular resilience declines, work backward through the model. Is oxidative stress elevated? Is inflammation persistent? Are cellular defense, mitochondrial function, or repair pathways underperforming? Then consider which modifiable inputs may be contributing.

Intervene upstream when possible. Reduce sources of cellular stress while supporting nutrition, the microbiome, and activation of the pathways involved in defense, energy, and repair. Rather than addressing each system in isolation, the goal is to restore the conditions that allow cells to function more effectively.

The model supports care—it does not replace it. This model does not diagnose or treat disease. It provides a framework for understanding the biological systems that influence cellular resilience and can be used alongside appropriate medical evaluation and care.

This model organises mechanisms described in the peer-reviewed literature cited throughout this site. It compresses a great deal of interacting biology into a readable sequence and omits many feedback loops, individual variation, and the substantial overlap between these compartments. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
